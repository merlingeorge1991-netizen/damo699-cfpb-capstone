Reading this. Raising the threshold buys precision and pays for it in recall. At 0.30 the team finds 86% of untimely responses but opens fourteen files for every one it confirms. At 0.90 it opens two files per confirmation and finds under a fifth of them. There is no threshold that does both, so the choice belongs to whoever owns the review capacity, not to the model.

Scores are not probabilities. Training used 5:1 undersampling, which lifts the base rate about thirty-five-fold, so a score of 0.60 does not mean a 60% chance of an untimely response. Treat the numbers as ranks.

This is validation, not test. The 2025 test year was scored once, with the earlier feature set, and was not rescored. On that year recall at the frozen 0.60 threshold fell to 0.161 even though prevalence rose — the ranking transferred across the year boundary but the calibrated cut-point did not.
